## Expert Personality and Feedback

### Expert Personality

The personality questionnaire was designed to collect information on the expert's background and individual differences **without** recording any directly identifying data (e.g., name, email, or address).

It comprises two components:

1. **Socio-demographic and professional profile**  
   A short set of questions captures:
   - age  
   - native language  
   - main cultural influences (countries)  
   - gender  
   - the expert's typical approach to public speaking training  
     (visual, audio, textual, and other methods)

2. **Big Five – based personality questionnaire**  
   A brief Big Five–style instrument consisting of ten self-descriptive statements (e.g., *“is reserved”*, *“has a vivid imagination”*).  
   Each item is rated on a 5-point scale from **Strongly agree** to **Can't say**, with two items tapping into each of the five traits:
   - Extraversion  
   - Agreeableness  
   - Conscientiousness  
   - Emotional Stability  
   - Openness to Experience  
   (adapted from Courtois et al., 2020).

In our study, the expert was a **35-year-old female native speaker of French**, who reported **France** as her main cultural reference. She indicated using all three main approaches in her coaching practice:

- visual analysis (body language, posture)  
- audio analysis (rhythm, intonation, pauses)  
- textual analysis (wording, structure, topic presentation, narration)

On the Big Five items, she:

- **strongly agreed** that she *works conscientiously* and *is relaxed, handles stress well*  
- **somewhat agreed** that she *has a vivid imagination*, is *sociable, outgoing*, and has *little interest in artistic matters*  
- was **neutral** regarding being *reserved* and *generally trusting others*  
- **disagreed** that she *tends to criticize others*, *tends to be lazy*, or *is easily anxious*


### Expert Feedback on the Annotation Process

After the first five annotations and again at the end of the annotation process, we collected the expert's feedback on:

- understanding of the task  
- confidence in their answers  
- adequacy of response options  
- transcription quality  
- usefulness and clarity of the criteria.

Based on the first round of feedback, we:

- **added explicit criteria** for *Introduction* and *Conclusion*  
- **reformulated** the *Passive Voice* criterion  
- **re-annotated** the first five transcripts with the updated criteria.

The tables below summarise the quantitative and qualitative feedback **before** and **after** these adjustments.

In summary, the expert judged the **criteria set** as highly relevant for the *Ma Thèse en 180 secondes* context and moderately to strongly useful in more general public speaking settings. At the same time, they highlighted two practical limitations:

- the **quality of automatic transcriptions** (even with a large Whisper model), which may affect the reliability of text-based evaluation;  
- the need for **finer-grained response options** for some criteria to better capture nuanced assessments.
